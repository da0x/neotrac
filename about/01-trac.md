# Thank you, Trac

About twenty years ago, I launched my first piece of software, and I did it with
[Trac](https://trac.edgewall.org).

Most of all, Trac got me over the finish line. Anyone who has shipped software knows
the last 5% of the work: the small fixes, the loose ends, the things that are easy to
lose track of and impossible to launch without. Trac organized all of it. Every piece
was a ticket, and every ticket wore its priority in color, so at a glance I could see
what had to be done before release and what could wait. At the time, I couldn't find
anything else like it. With it, I launched.

Trac gave my work a shape: tickets I could open and close, a timeline of everything
that had happened, milestones on a roadmap I could actually reach. It was plain, quick
and honest. It didn't try to manage me; it showed me the work, clearly, and stayed out
of the way.

I've never forgotten that. Much of how I think about building software, small steps,
every change written down, a history anyone can read, I learned with Trac open in a
browser tab. I'm grateful to the people who made it and gave it away.

## Why neotrac

Trac was built by [Edgewall Software](https://www.edgewall.org) and a community of
contributors, and given freely to everyone. It helped a great many projects find
their footing, mine among them. neotrac is my way of giving something back: the ideas
that made Trac good, rebuilt for the way we work now.

- **Every change kept.** An issue's history is its story, and the timeline is the
  project's.
- **Open by default.** A public project can be read by anyone.
- **Your team's own workflow.** Boards, phases and who moves what, set on the page,
  with nothing to deploy.
- **Free and open source,** under the AGPL, so it stays that way for everyone.

neotrac isn't part of Trac or Edgewall Software. It's a thank-you.
